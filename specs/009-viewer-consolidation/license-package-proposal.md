## Alternatives for owner decision

1. **Adapt selected Cubistry viewer code**, retaining identified CubeAssembler-derived material and notices. Price: extraction from dependencies on `@cubistry/cube-core`, `cube-mechanics`, and `cube-assets`, API adapters and parity work. Risk: AGPL-covered source and its distribution with EUPL-target core and MIT CubeAssembler require a specific legal decision; a separate package or thin adapter alone does not establish compatibility. Three.js notices and transitive artifacts need an inventory.
2. **Adapt selected CubeAssembler MIT source** into the shared viewer. Price: reconcile its app coupling and extend it for Cubistry's existing rendering and interaction needs; preserve source attribution and license. Risk: actual combined distribution with core and each consumer remains UNKNOWN; MIT source provenance does not decide the resulting package license or permit dropping third-party notices.
3. **Adapt selected CubeLab renderer**. Price: native WebGL and ReScript integration and substantial parity work against the established target; its app viewport stays in service until separately approved M3. Risk: AGPL-covered source, selected dependencies and any derived third-party source need legal review; no claim that its solver notice necessarily applies to the renderer.
4. **Keep current implementations while licensing and extraction are unresolved.** Price: duplicated maintenance and later integration; no shared viewer delivery yet. Risk: drift continues. This preserves all current consumers. It does not authorize a new third renderer, empty package, code copy, or migration.

Recommendation for the next decision: retain current implementations until an owner selects a candidate provenance and distribution objective, and assess technical and licensing compatibility for that concrete combination. This is not a license selection. The owner must decide separately: (a) permitted source candidate(s), (b) intended viewer package license and whether MIT CubeAssembler distribution must remain possible, (c) deployment/distribution scenarios to review, and (d) whether to authorize the later implementation phase after review. Each answer changes the scope and cost of the review; no compatibility guarantee is assumed.

## Acceptance gate for a future license review

A reviewable compatibility report identifies exact source commits and file-level origins (including modifications and contributors/rights-holder authority), proposed target package license, all direct and bundled transitive dependencies and notices, and actual source/binary/web distribution and network-use scenarios per consumer. It assesses the EUPL core LC decision, viewer terms and third-party obligations together, records conclusions and unresolved questions, and specifies required LICENSE/NOTICE, source availability, attribution and packaging evidence for each artifact. Owner decides the outcome and any need for external legal advice before implementation. External legal review is recommended if rights or compatibility remain unclear. No legal review has been performed here; compatibility, rights to relicense, and the required obligations are UNKNOWN.
